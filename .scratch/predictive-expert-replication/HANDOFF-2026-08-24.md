# Predictive expert replication — conclusions, 2026-08-24

Measured on 8× RTX 5090 (no NVLink, PCIe Gen5 x16) with Qwen3-30B-A3B, DP=8, EP=8,
`allgather_reducescatter`, eager mode.

Method, caveats and per-session detail are in `bench/RESULTS.md`; per-ticket
acceptance criteria are in `issues/`. This file is only the conclusions.

---

## The verdict

**Not worth building on this node with this model.** Perfect expert balance is
worth **0.08% to 0.15% of a decode step**, against 177 µs to move one 9.00 MiB
expert. Measured at four concurrencies (8 / 64 / 192 / 384), consistent across all.

Tickets `00` `01` `02` `03` `06` are closed. `04` `05` `07` `08` are correctly
specified but **not recommended here** — starting one needs an explicit decision,
not a ticket pickup. `09` is unstarted.

## Why — and it is not what the design worried about

`moe_align_block_size` pads every expert's token list up to a multiple of
`BLOCK_SIZE_M`. An expert therefore costs `ceil(its_tokens / BLOCK_SIZE_M)` blocks,
so **imbalance smaller than one block is free**. Balancing it saves nothing,
because the imbalance was never costing anything.

Measured consequence: token imbalance is 1.66×, but MoE **time** imbalance across
ranks is only **1.035×–1.06×**. The gap is quantization, not damping.

Imbalance starts to cost only when

```
M × topk / num_experts  >  BLOCK_SIZE_M

M            = decode tokens in flight after the allgather
BLOCK_SIZE_M = 16 (M≤32), 32 (M≤96), 64 (M≤512), 128 (M>512)
               for bf16 with no tuned config for the device
```

Qwen3-30B-A3B is 128 experts at top-8, so it needs **M > 2048**. This node's KV
serves about **464** at a 3072-token context. Short by ~4.4×, and unreachable.

**This inequality is the portable result.** It settles any other model shape by
arithmetic, with no hardware: fewer, larger experts reach the threshold sooner —
halving the expert count doubles tokens per expert.

## Three candidate bottlenecks, each ruled out by its own measurement

| Suspected | Measured |
| --- | --- |
| Placement policy | An optimal policy still recovers only ~0.15%: cost barely depends on token count |
| Interconnect | Collectives are ~20× the expert GEMM, but they carry token dispatch/combine, whose volume is set by token count and **not** by expert placement. Per-rank collective time does not track per-rank MoE time |
| Prediction accuracy | recall@2 is 0.74–0.78 at the default lookahead — good enough to act on |

MoE expert GEMM is **1.44%–3.56% of a decode step** across concurrency 8→384. Even
eliminating all imbalance touches only a fraction of that.

## Prediction accuracy — stands on its own, and transfers

recall@2 (the figure a planner depends on, since `max_replicas_per_layer` is 2):

| domain / shape | lookahead 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| code, 1k prompt | 0.828 | 0.781 | 0.726 |
| text, 2k prompt | 0.793 | 0.740 | 0.696 |

- Degradation is monotone, ≈5 points per layer of distance.
- **`prediction_lookahead_layers = 2` confirmed.** Lookahead 1 is ~5 points better
  but hides only 15% of a transfer, against 92% at lookahead 2. Lookahead 3 costs
  5 more points and buys nothing.
- **`prediction_skip_first_layers = 3` confirmed**, one layer conservative. Target
  layers 2 and 3 are categorically unusable (0.028 and 0.262 against a 0.750
  median); layer 4 onward is normal. Skip 2 is what the unambiguous evidence
  supports; 3 is the safer reading.
- Code predicts 3–4 points better than conversational text at every distance —
  routing really is content-dependent.

## Also established

- Source-rank routing is output-equivalent to canonical-only execution.
- Reserved replica slots are inert: 384 inactive slots carried no routed load under
  real requests, verified against non-zero recorded traffic.

## Not established — do not let these drift into conclusions

- **8× H100 on this model.** HBM3 and 80 GB raise reachable `M` roughly 4×, to
  about 1800 — still under 2048. NVLink shrinks the collectives and so raises MoE's
  *share*, but does not touch the inequality. **This is arithmetic, not
  measurement**, and it is the weakest claim here. Re-derive it before anyone
  spends money on hardware.
- **Whether early-layer inaccuracy belongs to the source or the target layer.**
  Targets 2 and 3 are both the earliest targets and the ones fed by the earliest
  sources; the runs cannot separate them. Both attributions imply the same action.
- **What the collectives actually spend their time on.** Byte volume is two orders
  of magnitude too small to explain it, so it is latency and arrival skew — but the
  proportions were not determined.
- **Under-load interconnect bandwidth.** The cost profile still carries the *idle*
  figure. Ticket `09` owns replacing it and needs the 8-GPU node.
- **Ascend.** Its expert count, top-k and block size change the arithmetic. Re-ask
  the question there rather than inheriting this answer.

## The one open direction

Probing a different MoE shape against the inequality above. It needs no new
hardware and no changes to the feature, and it is more likely to change the verdict
than new hardware is.
